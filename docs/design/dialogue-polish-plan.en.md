# Line polishing planning: style review based on character tone

Date: 2026-09-26. Undertake the "Reference Comparison Polishing" section of [Full Text Localization](translation-plan.md).

## Conclusion

- The machine-translated lines (45,013 lines in Chinese and English) are already reliable at the **meaning level**: 366 lines were compared line by line with the English manual translation of Serenes Forest. Our translation did not have meaning errors, but the reference made five or six errors; we scanned the entire database with more than 100 common idioms and hit 17, all of which were correctly translated. The payoff of finding errors line by line is low.
- The remaining gap is the **character tone**: the aristocratic lady, the old housekeeper, the hot-blooded boy, the soldier, and the old villain have distinct differences in personal pronouns, sentence endings, and honorific levels in Japanese. The Chinese and English translations are both neutral and consistent. The main body of this round of polishing is: first organize the character settings according to the work, and then let the model reread each line with the settings, only changing the tone, title, and tone without changing the meaning.
- There is another type of local misreading that needs to be checked separately: turning the page to cut a sentence (especially an idiomatic expression) in the middle, and the model only looks at half of the sentence (17831 "渜のscaleでも｜せんじて Drinking ませて"). There are 9,930 page-turning lines in the total library of 45,176 items, including 8,885 lines with no punctuation at the end of the page and sentences that span two pages. This category is covered by "entire line rereading".

## 1. Character setting (`content/translation/voices.json`)

From two sources, verify each other:

1. **Work setting**: The character’s identity, age, personality, and relationship with the protagonist in the original work. Organized by Claude according to 15 works participating in the war, based on his own understanding of the original works; uncertain markings are `status: "check"`, leaving it to the user for confirmation.
2. **Original text statistics**: `tools/translation/speaker_features.py` Count each speaker's personal pronoun, dual person, sentence ending, honorifics and swear words from all Japanese lines (plot + battle), and write it to `assets/translation-runs/voice/speaker-features.{json,md}`. This is direct evidence of the muzzle, e.g.マナミ「あたし＋のよ／だわ／かしら」、アイシャ「わたくし＋ですわ／わね」、ギャリソン「ですな／ございます」, マスター「ワシ＋様」, ジャネラ「じゃ／のじゃ」. The tone description in the setting must match the statistics.

Scope and stratification: There are 259 plot speakers, of which 202 with ≥20 lines cover 97% of the lines.

| --- | --- | --- |

Chinese principle (consistent with [proper name selection](../native/localization-terms.md), only mainland customs are used): all personal pronouns are "I", only the old man/old villain ワシ/わし uses "老夫", the emperor does not use "朕" (not in the original text), オイラIt is not translated as "I"; the level of honorifics is reflected in sentence patterns and titles ("you" is only given to speakers who have clear honorifics: housekeepers, subordinates to superiors, young people to elders); aristocratic accent relies on phrases such as "please/excuse/no need" rather than classical Chinese.
English principles: There is no difference in personal pronouns, and the tone of voice depends entirely on sentence structure, contraction, title and honorific words: Lawrence/Garrison uses "my lady/Master Banjo" without contraction for the master; military subordinates use sir/ma'am; senior villains use complete sentences without contraction, and occasionally use "you fool"; hot-blooded young people use contraction, exclamation, slang but no swear words; noble ladies do not use gonna/wanna.

Card fields: `work`, `gender`, `role`, `persona`, `ja` (statistical summary), `zh`, `en`, `address` (referred to as specific people, e.g.マナミ → ローレンス "Lawrence", ローレンス → マナミ "Miss/my lady"), `status` (draft/approved/check). `Characters.card()` will bring `zh`/`en` and `persona` into the character card of the prompt word.

## 2. Style review (`run_mt.py style`)

- Input: Japanese full page of a line, full page of existing translation, speaker card, name of conversation partner (speaker in the previous and following lines), scene title. Divide them into batches according to scenes, with each batch containing about 30 items. Try to use the same speaker or the same conversation in the same batch.
- Instructions: The meaning, proper name, placeholder, and page number remain unchanged; only change when the **tone** does not match the card, the **title** does not match the address, the **tone intensity** does not match the original text (exclamation, prolongation, stuttering), or the **respective level** is wrong (using a common tone to the superior); the changes should be as small as possible; write a reason for each change. Read the entire article, and understand the entire sentence without punctuation at the end of the page.
- Model: `deepseek-v4-pro-0813`; run Chinese and English separately; output `zh-v2-style`/`en-v2-style` (stage=style), `write_dialogue.py --runs` is stacked after the existing batch.
- The mechanical check follows `check_item` (page number, placeholder, punctuation, translation); two additional consistency checks are added: the same speaker's personal pronoun is unique in Chinese ("I" or "老夫"), and the occurrence rate of the title specified in the address table in sentences containing the name.
- Differences from the existing review (`review`): review finds errors, style only cares about the sound; neither uses reference translated sentences.
